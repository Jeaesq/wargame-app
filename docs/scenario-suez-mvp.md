# Suez Crisis MVP Scenario

## Scenario goal

This scenario proves the engine can support a second crisis with a different strategic texture from Berlin. The core pressure is not just superpower signaling. It is the interaction of canal access, post-imperial legitimacy, international backlash, and political timing.

## Scenario assumptions

- The player count is one or two
- One faction is the Anglo-French Coalition
- One faction is the Egyptian Republic
- Each turn represents a meaningful political-military interval rather than a day-by-day battle log

## Strategic texture

- The coalition wants leverage fast, but time works against it politically
- Egypt wants to preserve sovereignty and raise the diplomatic cost of intervention
- Public legitimacy and international pressure matter almost as much as raw military tempo
- Economic disruption around the canal can change incentives for both sides

## Public tracks

- canal control
- international pressure
- military tempo

## Private pressures

- coalition unity and outside pressure on London and Paris
- Egyptian regime cohesion and regional political support

## Outcome shape

- coalition success through regained leverage without intolerable backlash
- Egyptian success through sovereignty retention and diplomatic reversal
- partial settlements, stalemates, de-escalation, or crisis failure through uncontrolled escalation

## Design purpose

This scenario is meant to validate that scenario-specific objectives, opening states, narrative context, and option catalogs can all vary cleanly while the same turn engine, provider contracts, persistence, and solo flow remain intact.
